## 12. Content Advisory And The Age Setting

Each book carries an advisory minimum age set by its author, and the library hides books whose advisory age is higher than the age the app has for you. There are two ways it gets one. On devices that offer it — iOS 26 and later, and Android where Google Play supports it — the app asks the platform which **age range** you fall into, and you decide on your own device whether to share it; the app keeps the bottom of that range. Everywhere else you pick one of three ranges yourself — 13–15, 16–17, or 18 or over — and nothing checks it; on the devices that do offer a platform range there is a link on the same screen for picking it yourself **instead of being asked**, so nobody has to be looked up. If you decline the platform's own sharing prompt, the app does not put the picker up in its place — it assumes 13 and moves on, rather than asking you the same question twice. Either way it stays on your device. Where no range was shared you can set or change the age in Settings at any time, including after declining; **where a range was shared, that range is what the app goes by and there is no picker** — a range you think is wrong is corrected in your phone's own account, and then re-read with "Check my age range". If the app has no age, it assumes 13.

Please understand what this is and is not:

- **A range shared by Apple or Google is established by them, not by us.** This app performs no age check of its own: it asks for no birth date, no identity document and no account details, and it sees only the range and a label describing how it was arrived at. Where no range is available, the age is **self-declared and not verified** — nothing checks it.
- The per-book age ratings are **advisory guidance from the author**, not a certification, and not equivalent to a film or game classification.
- The filter is a **convenience feature, not a parental control, and not a substitute for a parent's or guardian's own judgement.** Where no range has been shared, you set the age yourself in Settings and nothing checks it. **Where a range has been shared, the app acts on nothing above the bottom of that range** — wherever you are, and whatever number may be stored beside it — because a range established by your phone's own store is a better answer than one anybody can pick for themselves. This changed on 14 August 2026: until then the range applied only where a law requiring an age check said so, and a number set in the app overrode it everywhere else.
- **Separately, where the platform tells the app a law requiring age assurance applies to you and no range has been shared, the app acts on nothing above 13**, whatever higher age is set in Settings. Your own number is still stored and still shown to you; it is simply not acted on above that limit. The app does not work out where you are in order to decide this; it relies only on what the platform tells it. **Clearing the app's data does not lift that limit.** The clear-local-data control erases the age and the range, but the app keeps the fact that the platform said an age check is required where you are, precisely so that clearing cannot be used as a way around this; only the platform telling the app otherwise removes it.
- **That second limit operates only where the platform actually tells the app, and the platform does not always tell it.** Where the app is not told and no range was shared, nothing is capped and an age you set yourself stands in full — which is most devices in use today. On iPhones and iPads below iOS 26 there is no such signal at all. On iOS 26.0 and iOS 26.1 the phone offers an age range but not that signal, so on those two releases the 13 limit never applies; from iOS 26.2 the phone answers the question and the app acts on the answer, including a "no", which lifts it again. On Android, Google Play tells the app one thing here and only in one situation — that age verification is required and you have not yet completed it with the store. Play has no signal meaning the opposite and stops saying this once you have completed the verification, so on an Android device where Play has never reported an outstanding verification the 13 limit never applies, and where it has, the app keeps that answer. These limits are stated rather than left out, because describing a protection that does not always operate would be worse than saying where it stops.
- **Checking again does not undo an age you set before your phone had answered.** Using "Check my age range" in Settings records whatever your phone says at that moment, but where you had set the age yourself it leaves your number in place rather than replacing it. Where the age came from your phone in the first place, the newer range replaces it. The app then works to whichever of the two is lower, so a stored number below the range is what stands, and a stored number above it does nothing — Settings offers a single control for adopting the range instead, which is the only thing that can raise what the app acts on. **"Newest" means the most recent range your phone actually confirmed to this app, which is not necessarily a recent one.** The app never asks your phone again by itself and the range it holds has no expiry, so a range confirmed long ago goes on setting the limit until a successful check replaces it — and a check that fails, or that you decline, leaves the older range in place rather than clearing it.
